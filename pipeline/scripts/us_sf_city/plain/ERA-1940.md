
RECORDS FROM ABOUT 1940 ON — READ THIS BEFORE YOU START.
The scan is clean, so no word needs guessing. The difficulty is different:

A. A REFERRAL IS NOT A DECISION. Modern minutes state the status in capitals:
   "REFERRED to the Land Use Committee", "RECEIVED AND ASSIGNED to ...",
   "CONTINUED to July 12", "TABLED", "Supervisor X requested that this matter be
   referred to Committee". In every one of those the Board has NOT decided. Write
   what actually happened:
     BAD : Chain pet shops banned on Geary Boulevard
     GOOD: Ban on chain pet shops sent to committee
     BAD : The Chautauqua House made a landmark
     GOOD: Landmark status proposed for the Chautauqua House
   If the record carries both a proposal and a final vote, the vote is the event.

B. MODERN VOCABULARY, in plain words:
   formula retail -> chain stores; Type 47 licence -> a restaurant liquor licence;
   Type 48 -> a bar liquor licence; Final Map -> a subdivision map; conditional
   use authorization -> special permission; landmark designation -> landmark
   status; encroachment permit -> permission to build into the street; street
   vacation -> closing a street for good; inclusionary housing -> below-market
   flats required in new buildings; Mitigated Negative Declaration -> a finding of
   no major environmental harm.
   Never print a code section ("Section 781.4", "Article 10") as if it were the
   event — say what the section DOES.

C. SOME ITEMS HAVE NO PLACE IN SAN FRANCISCO. Resolutions urging support for a
   state bill ("AB 22 (Lieber)"), or congratulating someone, are still real items.
   Write them honestly and do not invent a San Francisco location for them:
     GOOD: Board urges the state to pass an anti-trafficking bill

D. VERY SHORT ITEMS ARE STILL WRITABLE. "Motion No. M11-7 APPROVED 101583 [Final
   Map 5955: 1816 Eddy Street]" is a subdivision map approved at an address:
     GOOD: Subdivision map approved for 1816 Eddy Street

E. NAMING PEOPLE IN RECENT RECORDS. Organisations are always named. So are people
   acting in a public role — Supervisors, the Mayor, department heads — and people
   the Board is publicly honouring ("Certificates of honor voted for Dr. Carlton B.
   Goodlett" is right).
   But a PRIVATE individual in a record from 1975 or later is not named. Someone who
   applied for a permit, appealed a decision, or owns a house is a private person,
   and the line will sit on a public map beside their address. Use their role:
     RECORD: letter from the attorney for appellant, Mrs. Young Ngook Gay Lee,
             advising that she was withdrawing her appeal
     BAD   : Mrs. Young Ngook Gay Lee drops her 1400 Jones Street appeal
     GOOD  : The appeal over 1400 Jones Street is withdrawn and filed
   Before 1975 the older rule stands and private individuals are named — those
   records are history and the names are what make them readable.

F. NEVER OPEN WITH THE INSTRUMENT. "Motion…", "Resolution…", "Ordinance…" as the
   first word spends the most valuable position in the line on the paperwork type
   rather than on what happened. Measured on the first modern pass, 12% of lines
   opened this way — against nearly none in the older records.
     BAD : Motion to uphold the environmental report for 555 Washington Street
     GOOD: Board upholds the environmental report for 555 Washington Street
     BAD : Motion to write findings against the 935-965 Market Street report
     GOOD: Board moves to reverse the 935-965 Market Street report
     BAD : Environmental exemption for 1846 Grove Street put to the Board
     GOOD: Board asked to confirm 1846 Grove Street needs no review
   Say what happened. DO NOT name the Board as the actor: every record in this
   corpus is the Board's, so "Board approves…" spends two of your eleven words on
   the one fact the reader already has. Use the plain passive instead.
     BAD : Board approves the brew-pub licence at 620 Treat Avenue
     GOOD: Brew-pub licence approved at 620 Treat Avenue
     BAD : Board votes $1,350 for a safety station at Third and Market
     GOOD: $1,350 voted for a safety station at Third and Market
   Name a board ONLY when it is a different one — "Board of Education asked for a
   site" — or when the Board itself is what the record is about: "Board adjourns to
   meet in August" is right, because there the Board IS the event.
   "Put to the Board" is vague filler — say what is being asked for.
