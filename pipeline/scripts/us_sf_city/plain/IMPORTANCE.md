# How much a decision matters

One number per record, 0–100, read from the written line and its record.

This is a RUBRIC, not a pattern. Four rounds of ranking established that the
thing being judged cannot be matched lexically, and one attempt to try anyway
scored WORSE than the formula it replaced — −0.112 against +0.229 on the round
where money was held out. The reason is always the same shape:

    a nurse-staffing emergency at San Francisco General   ranked 2nd of 18
    a dog hospital at 3200 California Street              ranked 10th of 16
    a radio contract at the veterans hospital             ranked 13th of 16

Every one of those says *hospital*. What separates them is how much of the city
they touch, and that has to be read.

## The scale

**80–100 — the city changes for a lot of people at once.**
A rule about what may be built or banned where people live. Money into
something people use daily and would notice the loss of: a hospital, a library,
a school, a clinic, transit, a shelter. A benefit or obligation applying to a
whole class of people. A public asset gained.

**55–79 — a real change, for a neighbourhood or a defined group.**
Housing built or funded, with people housed at the end of it. A street's use
changed in a way residents would feel. A public asset given away — real, but it
is a loss, and it ranks below the same asset gained.

**30–54 — one address, one building, one block.**
Money at a site with no stated beneficiary. A permit for a business. A
subdivision map. A financing instrument attached to an address: a bond or a
note is machinery, and it scores below the same money described as housing
people.

**10–29 — one party's business, or a gesture.**
A settlement, a tax refund, an assessment appeal, a claim: the city pays or
forgoes, and one party benefits. A renaming, unless the name is one the city
knows. A commendation or a proclaimed week.

**0–9 — procedure with no subject.**
A file severed, rules suspended, a matter tabled with the matter unnamed.
A page carrying only a verdict and a roll call.

## Three adjustments, in the reader's own words

**Adopted weighs much more than proposed** — but stage discounts, it does not
kill. *"a proposal would rank high if it's something huge or out of the
ordinary… a large or significant change."* So a proposal that would change the
city noticeably keeps most of its score; a routine proposal loses roughly half.

**Money into daily-life infrastructure outranks a larger sum on a financing
instrument.** *"when money goes to like a hospital, some library, basically an
infrastructure of the city that matters daily life, I rank it higher than a
similar or a little higher amount on like a housing bond."*

**Housing and shelter sit near the top** — but *housing people* and *a housing
bond at an address* are not the same thing, and only the first belongs there.

## What the money is worth

A sum raises the score inside its band; it does not move a record between
bands. $67.3M to buy one warehouse is still one address. $3.7M to house
homeless young people reaches people. The reader ranked the second above the
first, and this is the rule that says why.

## Do not

* Do not score the topic label. Health ranked last of eight topics in one round
  and second of eighteen in another; the label carried nothing.
* Do not reward unusual wording. Measured across a full round: it counts for
  very little, and it promotes charming trivia over consequence.
* Do not reward recency. The year panel shows one year at a time, so records
  compete only against their own year. A recency thumb on the scale would empty
  the historic decades of anything worth reading.
* Do not invent reach the record does not state. If the line says a permit at
  an address, it is an address, however important the building sounds.
