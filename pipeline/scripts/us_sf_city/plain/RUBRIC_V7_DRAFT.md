Written while the v6 wave was still out. Nothing here has touched IMPORTANCE.md.
Applying it bumps RUBRIC_VERSION to 7 and obliges a re-read of everything scored
at v6, so each entry below carries the one thing that justifies that cost:
whether it moves a record ACROSS a band, and how many records a batch it touches.

Two entries deliberately fail that test and are marked. They ride along because
the file is already being opened, not because they earn a re-read of their own.

## 1. Delete the sentence that contradicts the thoroughfare fix

WITHIN BAND (34 vs 40). Rides along.

"Which streets are major" still requires a named length for the 40 to fire; "Four
fixes to anchors that were wrong" says to drop exactly that requirement. Both are
mine. Five readers have now hit it, and under one reading it fires 4 times in 113
lines, under the other zero.

    DELETE from "Which streets are major":
      "...and 40 fires only where a listed street is the subject and a length
       is named."

## 2. Third is a STREET

WITHIN BAND for most lines, but it is a rule error rather than a judgement, and
it names the wrong road in a way a reader can see is wrong. Rides along.

"Read the list as avenues where the city has one" makes Third Avenue — a Richmond
side street — the arterial, and demotes Third Street, which carries the rail line
and is the subject of lines like "Third Street rezoned from factories to
neighbourhood shops". Ocean and Nineteenth come out right; Third comes out
backwards.

    REPLACE "read the list as avenues where the city has one" with:
      "Third, Mission, Market, Van Ness, Geary and Folsom are STREETS.
       Ocean and Nineteenth are the avenues. Where both a street and an
       avenue carry the name, the listed one is whichever the line's own
       cross-streets place there; if the line does not say, take the street."

## 3. One floor, replacing two half-floors

CROSSES A BAND. ~14 records a batch on the permit half alone, plus every
settlement, advisory body, proclamation and motion-inside-an-appeal in the corpus.

Today "below 30, an anchor is not discounted" protects the 18 extension anchor and
stops there. Two separate defects fall out of that: "Chas. Roof ASKS to set off
blasts" halves the 33 permit anchor to 17 while "Chas. Roof ALLOWED to set off
blasts" stays 33 — same permit, two spellings, a band apart; and an unanchored act
with a real subject (a settlement, a named advisory body) rides the ladder down
into 0-9, the band reserved for procedure with NO subject.

One rule closes both:

    REPLACE "Below 30, an anchor is not discounted" with:
      "The ladder never carries an act below the band its subject earns.
       An anchored act does not fall below its own anchor. Any act with a
       named subject stays at 10 or above — 0-9 is for procedure with no
       subject in it, and a proposal, an appeal or a referral is not that."

## 4. Give street WORK the split street LEVELS already has

CROSSES A BAND (38 -> 44). 52 of 292 street-work lines in one measured batch.

Street levels got a 34/40 thoroughfare split. Paving, grading, sewering and
sidewalk work — four times the population — got none, so "Mission Street repaved
from Fourth to the Embarcadero" ties a fifty-foot patch on an alley at 38.

    ADD to the street-work anchor:
      "38 where the subject is an ordinary street; 44 where a listed
       thoroughfare is the subject. Length is not required."

## 5. Floor the citywide-component multiplier

CROSSES A BAND. ~46 records a batch in the Hetch Hetchy and Municipal Railway
years.

"A component of a citywide system takes the system's score less a third" has no
lower bound, so "$36 more toward a Heisler locomotive" takes 53 and outranks
"$29,311.05 for San Francisco Hospital brickwork" at 45. A trivial material for a
citywide system should not outrank a large trade on a civic building.

    ADD:
      "The component rule raises a score; it never carries a small material
       purchase above what the same sum would earn on a single building.
       Where the sum is what the line is mostly about, score the sum."

## 6-8. The three populations with no anchor

CROSSES A BAND, all three. Together 41 to 258 records a batch. These are the
largest holes in the rubric and the reason every reader invents a private ladder.

The numbers below are batch 035's. Batch 043's reader independently stated its
own ladder for the same three families, and every comparable rung landed in the
SAME BAND — largest gap 5 points (see RUBRIC_OPEN.md). So these numbers are
corroborated at band level before anyone has tested them, which is the level the
rubric actually decides at.

The lease FORK is a different matter and the ladder does not close it. Both those
readers scored a space by what it houses; the 42-point disagreement in the 017
double-read was between that reading and one that scored it as an office because
the clerk wrote "office". Rule 7 below has to settle which reading wins — check
it first after v7 lands.

    ADD three anchors:

    Traffic and parking regulation
      one street or block                                        38
      a corridor on a listed thoroughfare, or a one-way
        or turn-ban conversion                                   44
      a district-wide or citywide rule                           58
    (This also settles the two sections that currently claim these lines at
     different bands: they are 30-54 unless the rule is district-wide.)

    A lease of space the public uses
      neighbourhood service — clinic, childcare, branch
        library, shelter                                         55
      court or hearing quarters                                  40
      department office                                          26  (existing)
    Score what the space HOUSES. Where the line says only "office" but names
    a department the public visits, it is not an office for this purpose.

    Landmark or historic designation
      one building                                               42
      a district                                                 62
    A designation is a permanent rule, not a permit: the proposal discount
    applies, but rung 1 only.

## 9. The appeal keeps its subject

CROSSES A BAND. 164-172 records, 14% of a modern batch.

Every appeal currently flattens to 16 regardless of what is under appeal, so
"Appeal heard over 203 low-income flats" ties "Appeal heard over gym locker
rooms". The appeal is a stage, and stage is a discount — not a replacement for
the subject.

    ADD:
      "An appeal, a rehearing or a continuance is a rung on the ladder, not
       a subject. Score what is being appealed, then discount one rung.
       Where the line does not say what is under appeal, it is a line with
       no subject and scores as procedure."

## 10. The clerk's wording must not decide the band

CROSSES A BAND. 8-14 records a batch measured, and the shape is general.

"$71M in housing bonds proposed for Mason and Eddy Streets" scores 18;
"$75M in bonds proposed for 137 flats at 4840 Mission Street" scores 51. The
machinery discount fires only when no unit count is written, so the band turns
on whether the clerk mentioned the flats.

    ADD to the machinery discount:
      "Housing bonds, site acquisition and financing for a named residential
       site are housing, whether or not a unit count is written. The
       machinery discount is for money moved with no stated destination."

## 11. A subject with no act

CROSSES FOUR BANDS (8 vs 48). ~17 records a batch, every modern batch.

"Board adopts Resolution No. 571-10 for the Transbay Transit Center" names a large
subject and no act at all.

    ADD:
      "Where a line names a subject but no act, score the subject one rung
       below what the smallest plausible act on it would earn. Do not score
       it as procedure — the subject is stated — and do not borrow the act
       from the line above it."
